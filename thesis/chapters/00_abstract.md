Stress is difficult to notice early, and most wearable stress detectors judge every wearer against one population threshold. This individual component of the MindPulse multimodal system develops a personalised stress detector for a low-cost smart ring that carries a pulse sensor (MAX30102), a galvanic skin response (GSR) sensor and an accelerometer. A binary Normal/Stress XGBoost classifier was trained on 913 sixty-second windows from the 15 subjects of the WESAD dataset, using 23 heart-rate-variability (HRV), electrodermal and motion features, and was chosen from ten classifiers under leave-one-subject-out (LOSO) validation, where it reached 85.1% accuracy and an AUC of 0.938 on raw features. Because the system scores each wearer against their own baseline, the model was then trained on baseline-normalised features; under LOSO validation this reached 94.5% accuracy, a macro F1 of 0.939 and an AUC of 0.986 at a ROC-derived threshold of 0.6447 (93.4% at the default 0.5 cut-off). Personalisation was built in layers: a 3.5-minute calm-breathing baseline, an eight-minute cognitive battery that sets a personal stress threshold only when the user's rest and task scores differ by at least 0.10, exponential-moving-average baseline updates at recalibration, a seven-day drift check, and a feature-voting trend indicator. The trend indicator detected stress onset in all 15 WESAD subjects within six windows, against 63% expected by chance, at an 18% false-alarm rate in calm windows. Checking the pipeline end to end exposed a mismatch between the first model's training features and its baseline-normalised inputs, which was corrected by retraining.
